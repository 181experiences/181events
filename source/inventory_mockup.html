Type what is on the shelf. Red means it is at or under the minimum, which is the reorder line, not an emergency. Leave a box empty if you could not get to it; the report says so honestly. Everything saves as you type, so the walk survives a phone going to sleep or a hand-off.

Rules the screen keeps. Minimums come from the item list, never typed here. Items that live in two places are two rows, one per place, so a counter never adds in their head. Pods: boxes and loose, the site does the ten-to-a-box math. The area note is where machine faults, resident feedback and “the closet was locked” go. Who typed each number is stamped, so a two-person count reads correctly on the report. On an iPhone: this screen is built for one hand; number boxes open the numeric keypad, every target is at least 44 pixels, one area opens at a time so the thumb never travels far, and the sticky bar keeps Hand off and Submit in reach.

The item list

What gets counted, where, and what the minimum is. Leadership keeps this table itself: Edit on any row opens it in place (name, minimum, unit, area, who orders it), Add an item puts a new row in an area, Remove retires a row without touching past counts. Areas are in walk order and can be moved; items too. Changing a minimum changes the next count, never a past one. Staff and owner edit; the desk reads.

Under the hood, if you say go. Three D1 tables: inv_items (name, area, walk order, minimum, unit, ordered-by, active), inv_counts (started/finished by and when, scope, status Open / Submitted / Closed, notes), and inv_lines (count, item, quantity, loose pods, who typed it, when). Autosave is a PATCH per keystroke-pause, so a hand-off loses nothing. The report is rendered once at submit, stored as HTML on the count row, and that same HTML is the email body and the printable page. Dev server mirrors every route. Two sessions, same playbook as Pages.

The printed sheet

For anyone who would rather walk with paper. Print a blank sheet renders the live item list, in walk order with today’s minimums, as a Letter page set. The sheet is a helper, not the record: whoever carries it types the numbers into a count on the site when they are back at a desk, or hands the sheet to someone who will. Every submitted report prints the same way, from its own Print button.

Why it stays a helper. A paper count the site never hears about cannot email anyone, cannot turn red, and cannot be picked up by the next shift. The sheet makes the walk comfortable for people who like a clipboard; the site remains the one place the count lives.
