Chapter one

What to expect

Walk the red carpet on arrival, then join us for an evening of food, drink, and merriment, from 6:30 until 9:00 at The Residents’ Club. Black tie if you have it, and glamour however you care to define it.

The red carpet

Arrive like it is a premiere. The rope, the carpet, the flashbulbs.

Live music

Music of the era, played live, all evening.

Cocktails

Coupes of champagne and the classics, poured the old way.

The table

Shrimp cocktail, a prime rib station, a side of salmon carved to order, and more as the evening unfolds.

The silent film lounge

Black and white on the big screen, low lamps, deep chairs, a place to land between dances.

Pictures

A photographer on the carpet, and your portrait to keep.

Chapter two

The look, decoded

Think the studio years, 1934 to 1955. Satin that catches light, black that swallows it, one piece of jewelry that does the talking. Nobody needs a costume; everybody is invited to lean in.

One · Just come

A dark suit or a cocktail dress

  • Add one Old Hollywood touch: a bow tie, a red lip, a brooch, a pocket square
  • Shoes polished, hair done
  • Honestly, this is plenty
Two · Lean in

Black tie, or a gown

  • Tuxedo, or a long dress in satin, velvet or sequins
  • Long gloves, pearls, cufflinks
  • A wave in the hair, a part on the side
Three · The full picture

The movie star

  • White dinner jacket, cummerbund, a fedora at the door
  • Bias-cut gown, fur stole (faux is perfect), finger waves, a feathered headband
  • Arrive on someone’s arm

Glamour, however you define it

The dress code speaks every language. A sari in silk and zari, a lehenga, a cheongsam in brocade, a sherwani: dressed to the nines in the 1940s was never only a tuxedo, and the evening is richer for every tradition in the room. Jewelry, the family pieces especially, is very much the point. You will find these looks all through the reels, where they belong.

Chapter seven

Questions, answered

Do I have to dress up?

No. Come as you are and you will be welcome. The evening rewards a little effort, and a single Old Hollywood touch is all it takes to join in.

Can I bring a guest?

This one is residents only, with no outside guests. The building’s own children are residents too, and welcome. Seats are limited, so RSVP by Monday, December 14.

Where do I find a tuxedo, or a gown, in time?

Rental is the easy road for black tie, and most houses deliver within the week. For gowns, rental and vintage both work, and the city’s vintage shops are made for this brief. Mockup note: Leo to add the places he trusts.

Will there be photographs?

On the red carpet, yes, and you will get yours. Inside, the evening is for the people in it; take all the pictures you like, and ask before you post someone else’s.

Is there anything I should not wear?

Costumes in the Halloween sense. This is glamour, not fancy dress: no plastic cigarette holders, no gangster props. A feather boa, on the other hand, is allowed.
