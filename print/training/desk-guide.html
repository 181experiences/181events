The front desk guide to 181residents.com

Residents use 181residents.com to see the calendar, RSVP, and write to Resident Experiences. The desk keeps two doors open at any hour, a fresh sign-in code and an RSVP taken by phone, and on private-party nights it runs the guest list. This guide walks through each; the one-page cheat sheet beside the desk phone is the short version.

1 · Signing in

Go to 181residents.com/admin. There is also a quiet Staff link at the foot of every page of the resident site; it lands in the same place, so the address never needs remembering.

  1. Enter concierge@181sf.com and ask for the emailed code.
  2. The code arrives in the desk mailbox. Type it in and you are through.
The first code often fails, and that is normal. Our mailboxes scan every link in an incoming email before you see it, and that scan uses the code up. If the page says the code has already been used, simply request a new one; the scanner does not check twice, so the second code works. Nothing is wrong and nobody is locked out.

You land on the Dashboard, signed in as front desk. Your tabs are Dashboard, Residents, Spaces, and Messages. The grey bar at the very top reports on the system itself; when everything is well it stays quiet.

Stay signed in. There is no need to sign out between shifts; the desk computer keeps its session, and each fresh sign-in costs another emailed code. Sign in once, and leave it be.

2 · How resident sign-in works

Every resident has a personal eight-character code, one per person; couples each have their own. A resident signs in once on the site and that device stays signed in for a month, so most people type their code a few times a year at most. There are no passwords to forget and no accounts to manage. The trade is that the desk becomes the keeper of codes, which is the next section.

3 · The lost code, any hour

The classic call comes at ten at night: an iPad updated, a new phone, a code card long gone. The whole rescue takes under a minute.

  1. Open the Residents tab and type a name or unit into the find box. The household’s card opens with the person in it. (The cards fold by unit; the find box opens the right one for you.)
  2. Press Rotate. A fresh code appears, and the old one stops working everywhere, on every device, right away. That is the point: a rotated code cannot wander.
  3. Read the new code over the phone, or press Email code, which opens a draft in Outlook with the code written out, ready to send from the desk mailbox.
"Of course. I will issue you a fresh code right now; the old one stops working the moment I do. Do you have a pen?"

Two things to hold to. Codes belong to people, not to households: if a resident asks for a partner’s code, rotate that person’s own row and pass the new code along. And a rotate signs that person out everywhere, so only rotate for the person who is actually asking.

Every row carries the same three buttons: Email code, Rotate, and Edit. A shaded Email code button just means no email is on file; Edit is where one gets added. A yellow Tenant pill marks a renter; it changes nothing about the rescue.

If the person is not in the list, or their row says Disabled, or their stay has an end date that has passed, take their name and number and pass it to Leo rather than guessing. The one exception you handle yourself is a temporary stay, below.

4 · Temporary stays

A renter or a visiting family member gets their own row rather than borrowing a code. Add them at the top of the Residents screen: unit, name, email if offered, and an Access ends date. The code simply stops working after that date, with nothing to remember to turn off.

Everything else about a person lives behind Edit on their row: the end date, their email, and the Disable button, which is the right tool when someone moves out because it keeps their history. Delete lives there too; leave it alone, it removes a person entirely and exists for typos.

5 · RSVPs by phone, or in passing

Some residents will always rather tell a person than tap a screen, and the desk is that person. On the Dashboard, in the RSVPs section, press Add an RSVP for someone: pick the person, the event, the party size (one to six), add names if they offer them, then Save RSVP. If they already had an RSVP for that event, saving simply updates theirs.

Capacity works exactly as it does on the site, so one queue stays one queue. If the event is full, or other parties are already waiting, the new RSVP joins the waitlist. Say so kindly, and plainly; nobody skips the line at the desk. An event can also close its RSVPs, by its closing date or by hand; after that, a new request goes to Resident Experiences as a request, and you may say exactly that.

"That dinner is full at the moment, so I have you next on the waitlist for two seats. The moment seats open, we will let you know."

One number worth knowing: on the site, residents book for themselves and up to two others. A bigger party is invited to contact us, and the desk is the us. You can seat up to six, which is how those larger parties get arranged.

Every RSVP row has Edit and Cancel. Edit opens the full card: change the party size or the names, or even move the RSVP to another event, and the new event’s own capacity rules answer. Cancel asks you first, then asks separately whether a note to the resident should go. Confirm seats appears on a waitlist row once seats have freed up; freed seats are never handed out on their own, and pressing it gives them to that party. After any change, an email to the resident opens prefilled in Outlook, ready to send from the desk mailbox. Read it, then send it. If there is no email on file, the screen says so honestly: a call, or a word in the lobby, closes the loop.

6 · Private-party nights: the guest list

When a resident hosts a private event in one of the spaces, their outside guests register themselves in advance, and the desk holds the door. Guests announce themselves by the event name, not the host’s unit.

  1. On the Spaces tab, open the evening’s reservation and its Guests panel. There is usually a printed list at the desk as well; they are the same list.
  2. As each guest arrives, press Arrived beside their name; a plus-one has their own line. On paper, tick the box.
  3. A name not on the list: check with the host before letting anyone through, then Add writes them in.

Everything else about the party, its registration page, its link, its cap, belongs to Resident Experiences; the desk’s part is the door.

7 · Messages

Notes sent from the site’s Message tile land in the Messages tab, with the promise of a reply within one business day. The desk view shows who wrote and when, and whether it has been answered; the text itself is private to Leo, because residents write to a person. If a resident mentions building maintenance or a service issue, point them to the front desk log or Action Life as usual; that inbox is for Resident Experiences.

8 · If something looks wrong

  • The grey bar at the top says what is unwell, in plain words.
  • First: reload the page.
  • Still wrong: call Leo, and read him what the grey bar says.
