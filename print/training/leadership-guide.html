The leadership guide to 181residents.com

The admin at 181residents.com/admin runs the resident calendar end to end: events and series, RSVPs and waitlists, the asset kits behind every poster and screen, and the reporting. This guide covers the staff view for Scott, Leigh Anne, and Carley-Ann. The one-page cheat sheet is the short version, and the admin’s own Settings tab holds the deeper reference documents.

1 · Signing in, and who sees what

Go to 181residents.com/admin, enter your own work email, and type in the code that arrives by email. The first code often comes back “already been used”; that is the mailbox’s link scanner using it up before you can. Request another and the second one works.

There are three views of this admin. Leo sees everything, including the text of resident messages. You see events, assets, residents, spaces, the dashboard, and message records. The front desk sees a working subset: codes, phoned-in RSVPs, spaces, and message records, with no event tools. Nothing needs configuring; the view follows the signed-in email.

Two small habits. On any shared machine, use Sign out at the top right when you finish. And if you use the admin often, your browser will offer to install it as its own app; it then opens from the dock or home screen like anything else.

2 · How the calendar reaches residents

The admin edits a database; the resident site is built from it. Press Publish calendar after a working session and the site rebuilds from what is saved. Scheduled rebuilds through the day keep dates rolling forward on their own, so yesterday’s events grey out and the Next Event tile stays honest without anyone touching anything.

The calendar window decides how far ahead residents see, with two dials atop the Events screen: full pages with RSVP out to a number of weeks, then quiet shaded dates marked “Details to come” out to a number of months, then nothing at all. The windows slide forward with every rebuild, so an event wakes up on its own as its date approaches, and plans further out stay yours to change.

For a single event whose details are still settling, tick Coming soon instead: it goes out as a published teaser, date and title showing, with RSVP and calendar buttons waiting until the box is unticked.

3 · Creating an event

On the Events tab, + New Event opens the editor. Most fields explain themselves; these carry the doctrine:

  • Category matches the budget line items, so the monthly report rolls up against what Scott already sees.
  • Count in engagement reporting: choose List only whenever the host is not Resident Experiences, so nothing credits us with someone else’s attendance.
  • RSVP type: Seat holds seats, Guest count collects outside-guest numbers only, and Paid seat shows the price with seats held; payment itself stays off the site.
  • Capacity left blank means no limit. RSVP closes is a real date, for example the Monday of the event week for workshops, so materials can be ordered against a firm count; after it, new requests come to Resident Experiences as requests rather than seats. Close RSVPs now, beside it, closes them this instant, and unticking reopens them.
  • The marquee checkbox features one event on the home screen.

4 · Series

The Repeats picker writes one row per date, which is the quiet trick behind everything series shaped: a single week can be moved, re-timed, or skipped without touching the rest. In the editor, Which date of this series chooses the week you are looking at, and the apply to every upcoming date box decides how far a change reaches. Untick it to change one date only. When a date moves, its sign-ups, counts, and feeds follow along; nothing is orphaned.

5 · Statuses, the editor’s verbs, and the Archive

  • Draft is never published.
  • Live is on the resident calendar.
  • Unpublished is pulled from the site with its RSVPs held, so an event can go back up unchanged.
  • Archived is over, hidden, and kept for reporting.

The editor moves between these with two buttons. Publish puts an event out and reads Unpublish once it is; Save draft saves without changing where the event stands. On a Live event, Save draft keeps your edits as a working copy residents never see: the editor says a draft is pending, Publish changes sends it out, and Discard draft lets it go. Nothing you type into a live event reaches residents until you say so.

Every save also lands in the event’s change history at the foot of the editor: who, when, and what changed. Load this version brings any earlier version back into the form for review; publishing or saving it is what makes it real again.

The Archive files itself: when an event’s last date passes, or it is cancelled, it drifts off Events and Assets into the Archive, kit and all. Downloads and Canva links stay live there, ready to serve as templates for the next season. Reach it from the Archive · N link atop Events or Assets; restoring is one edit, status back to Live, and it walks back out.

6 · Capacity, waitlists, and Confirm seats

A full event queues a waitlist, and it is one queue: the site, the desk, and this admin all feed the same line. Freed seats are never handed out automatically while anyone waits. On the Dashboard, Confirm seats appears on a waitlist row once seats free up; pressing it gives them to that party and opens a note to them, ready to send. A confirmed party never loses seats by editing their RSVP, and a request to grow only goes through if it fits.

RSVPs are parties. On the site a resident books for themselves and up to two others, and may list names; the fourth chip, Please contact me, sends a larger party to the Message page and the desk, and staff seat up to six from the Dashboard, which is how that promise is kept. The per-event rollup shows parties by unit; a unit appearing twice for one event is worth a glance, since a household can double-count itself.

7 · Handling RSVPs from the Dashboard

Add an RSVP for someone covers the resident who phones or asks in passing, and capacity answers the same as on the site. On any row, Edit opens the full card: change the party or the names, or move the RSVP to another event; a move books the new event under its own rules, then releases the old seats, and the note offered afterward names both. Cancel asks first, then asks separately whether a note should go. After any change, the note opens prefilled from your own mailbox; nothing sends itself.

On the day itself, Arrived beside each party marks attendance at the door: one tap marks the whole party in, a second tap asks for the true number when fewer came, and 0 clears the mark. The RSVP is never touched, so RSVPed and came stay two honest numbers; the CSV export carries them side by side in an Attendance table.

Passed events keep their lists under Past events at the foot of the RSVP list: who came, by unit, the arrival tally, and each party’s email in view. Email guests there opens one BCC draft to everyone who held seats, made for the thank-you note and the survey.

8 · Telling residents about changes

Change a Live event’s date, time, or location while people are signed up, and one BCC email opens in Outlook addressed to everyone affected, with old and new spelled out and a line about updating calendars. Read it, then send it; it goes from your own mailbox, on purpose, so nothing is sent in your name unseen. If someone signed up has no email on file, the screen says so, and a call or a word in the lobby closes the loop.

Cancel & notify guests, in the editor, is the graceful exit: it pulls the event from the site, holds the RSVPs, and opens the cancellation draft.

Three companions to know. Link, on any event row or series date, copies that date’s page address, made for pasting into emails and reminders. For something standing, every event also answers at a short address written by hand from its slug: 181residents.com/e/book-club always lands on the next upcoming date of that series, so a printed card never goes stale. And residents who subscribed to the calendar feed update automatically; those who used Add to My Calendar re-tap it once after a change, which the drafts already explain.

9 · Asset kits: masters and overrides

Every event carries a kit: hero image, Nixplay still, elevator sign, and so on, each row saying what the piece is and where it goes. Per piece there are two homes: the uploaded file is the final version, here for anyone to download on any device, and the Canva link beside it opens the living design for edits.

A series carries one master kit, under Assets, and every date inherits it. When one week needs its own artwork, open that date in the editor: the kit section shows what the date actually uses, and Override this date gives it its own file while the rest of the series stays on the master. Back to series kit hands it back. Every file is named from the event’s stem, so it stays identifiable in Canva, on Nixplay, or at the print shop. Specs live under Instructions, Screens & Print.

10 · Private events, and the guest list

A reservation on Spaces can carry a private event: a resident hosts, and their invited outside guests register themselves on one page, no resident code involved. Give the reservation an event name (guests announce it at the door), a host, and a cap if one is wanted, flip Open registration, and Copy registration link hands the host their invitation. An optional written address, such as 181residents.com/register/tang50, suits printed cards; written means guessable, so put a number in it. Print guest list makes the sheet the desk and security run from, and the desk marks arrivals on the night. Nothing about a private event appears on the resident calendar. The full walkthrough lives under Settings, Private events & guest lists.

11 · The Dashboard, and the monthly story

The Dashboard shows 7, 30, or 90 days: visits by day, where traffic came from (every QR standee and the weekly email carry their own link, so each scan is attributable), what residents open the site on, the live calendar by category, and the RSVP rollups. Download CSV exports the period for Excel.

The reporting doctrine, worth repeating in any board conversation: with a small year-round population, counts stand beside their percentages, and month-to-month swings read as noise until three months agree. Events hosted by others are listed but not counted, so the engagement figures only ever claim what Resident Experiences actually did.

12 · Messages

The Messages tab shows who wrote, when, and whether it has been answered, so response times are visible. The text itself is private to Leo: residents write to a person, not a department, and the inbox keeps that promise.

13 · Residents and codes, in brief

Sign-in codes are the desk’s daily work, and the desk guide covers the rescue routine. The staff view has the same Residents screen when needed: households fold into unit cards, the find box opens the right one by name, unit, or email, and every row carries Email code, Rotate, and Edit. Everything else about a person, their email, end dates for temporary stays, owner or tenant standing (a yellow Tenant pill marks renters), Disable for move-outs, lives behind Edit. Bulk add takes pasted lines of “unit, name, email, owner or tenant”, and it is safe to paste the whole directory: anyone already listed under the same unit and name is skipped with their code untouched, only new names get rows and codes, and a blank email or standing is filled in from the paste. Print code cards makes a sheet of cards at move-in or a re-issue.
